# Monthly screener review

Schedule: 1st of each month, 10:07 am ET (`CRON_TZ=America/New_York 7 10 1 * *`)
Data: the dashboards, plus prices from this repo's `data/` folder
Report-only: never changes the screener, engines or other tasks.

## Prompt

Write the monthly review that turns Cade's paper trading record and the screener's learning log into suggestions for his swing screener. Paper dashboard: https://claude.ai/artifact/BPQJfrJd3xztYfGUF5ZmV9 (ArtifactData collection "agent", doc_id "state" holds trades, positions and the daily equity record; collection "agent", doc_id "engine" holds the trading rules; collection "agent", doc_id "options_state" holds the calls version — a parallel book that mirrors every share trade with a ~0.70-delta, 90-120 day call sized to the same delta exposure, with modeled Black-Scholes prices). Screener dashboard: https://claude.ai/artifact/ULtzUMAxM2BaXZmpGbamzU. Screener rules: the scheduled task "Weekly swing trade screen" (rules version in the screener's ArtifactData collection "agent", doc_id "history_engine", field rules_version, with rules_changelog; score = 50% strength rank + 35% trend checks + 15% closeness to the 52-week high; gates: above 200-day, 50-day over 200-day, strength rank >= 80 (v2 since 2026-11-11; v1 used 60 and a 2-per-sector cap); no price stop in v2 (2.5 x ATR is the sizing reference; v1 used 2.5 x ATR stops trailing at 3 x ATR); the paper book holds the swing trades as a 30% sleeve with 70% in VOO; pullback if more than 12% above the 50-day; an insider flag from 90 days of Form 4 filings — Strong buy / Buy / Neutral / Caution / Unavailable — is recorded but does not affect the score). Use Python for every number. Do NOT change either scheduled task, the engines, the history engine, or the screener. Only report and recommend.

1. Load the state. Closed trades are in state.trades. Each has setup, r_multiple, ret_pct, excess_pct vs SPY, days_held, exit_reason, mfe_pct, mae_pct, and features at entry: score, rs_pct, trend, ext_50, off_high, eps_surprise, target_upside, sentiment, days_to_earnings, vix_regime, insider_flag, insider_buys_90d (trades entered before October 2026's insider update have no insider_flag; treat those as missing, not Neutral). Also look at open positions and the equity record vs the SPY benchmark. Load options_state too: options_state.pairs (closed trades done both ways), options_state.calls (open calls) and options_state.equity (daily calls-version value next to shares_equity).
Also load the screener learning log: ArtifactData get on the screener dashboard, collection "datasets", doc_ids "history" and "scorecard"; download each file with the Artifact tool (action read, url = screener dashboard, path = the 32-character id after /_blob/). screen_history.json has one row per stock per weekly screen (picked or not) with its inputs at the time (passes, finalist, rank, status, rs_pct, trend, ext_50, off_high, score, group, insider_flag, vix, mom_3m, stop, rules_version) and forward results (ret_4w/spy_4w, ret_8w/spy_8w, ret_13w/spy_13w, ret_26w/spy_26w as % changes, min_close, stop_hit). Returns are from weekly closes, so stop_hit and min_close miss intraweek moves.

2. Analyse:
(a) Overall: portfolio vs SPY, max drawdown of both, closed-trade count, win rate, average R, and expectancy (average R per trade).
(b) By setup (Actionable / Post-earnings / Pullback): count, win rate, average R.
(c) By feature, splitting at the median or into terciles: score, rs_pct, ext_50, eps_surprise, target_upside, vix_regime. Do higher values do better?
(d) Insider flag: compare trades flagged "Buy" or "Strong buy" against "Neutral", and list "Caution" trades separately: count, win rate, average R, average excess return vs SPY for each group. Leave out "Unavailable" and missing. Also check the screener's pick log (collection "datasets", doc_id "picks_log", then download its file) and the screen history: compare picks' return vs SPY by flag there too. Those samples grow faster than closed trades.
(e) Exits: how many stop-outs later went higher (get today's price for stopped symbols from Cade's GitHub repo: add_repo owner CadeBrek repo swing-screener, `git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/CadeBrek/swing-screener repo`, then repo/data/daily/latest/prices.json, falling back to repo/data/weekly/latest/prices_all.json; do not use Bigdata.com, which is out of credits), MFE vs final result (are the trailing stops giving back too much?), MAE of winners (would a tighter stop have survived?), and what time stops and screen-fail exits did.
(f) Watchlist outcomes: from state.events, how many "Unwatch" decisions (failed earnings check, fell through zone) and, where the price is now known, whether skipping them was right.
(g) Shares vs calls: from options_state.pairs, total and average call_pnl vs share_pnl, average call_r vs share_r, win rate of each, how many pairs the call did better, and average capital_saved. Split winners from losers (calls should lose less than shares on big drops and give up some gain to time decay on winners — check whether that holds). From options_state.equity: return since the book started for both books and max drawdown of each. Flag pairs with late_start = true (measured from the day the call was bought). Remember the call prices are modeled (volatility from ATR, fixed until a roll, no earnings IV crush, estimated spreads), so these are estimates.
(h) Screener learning log, using only rows with a result at that horizon (excess = ret - spy), at 4 and 13 weeks: finalists vs passed-not-picked vs failed the gates (is the screen adding anything?); among passers, excess by rs_pct band (60-69, 70-79, 80-89, 90-100), by trend points, by ext_50 band (<5, 5-12, >12) and by off_high band; finalists by status and by insider flag; finalists by vix at pick (<20 vs >=20) and mom_3m sign; and stop_hit rate of finalists. Then test the specific rule thresholds: would a strength gate of 70 or 80 have beaten 60, would a pullback cut at 8% or 15% have beaten 12%, and did the score order matter (rank 1-4 vs 5-8, and top-8 passers vs passers ranked 9-16)? For each comparison give n and average excess. Count each stock-week as one observation but note that weekly rows of the same stock overlap, so the effective sample is smaller; also report how many distinct stocks and distinct weeks are behind each number. Compare rules versions separately: trades and log rows carry their rules version (v1 until 2026-11-11, v2 after; all v1 positions were closed on the first v2 session with exit_reason "Switched to v2 rules", so leave those exits out of the exit analysis in (e)). Judge v2 against both SPY and the backtest's expectation for the 30/70 book (docs/v2-proposal.md). Also note the data source changed on 2026-11-16 from Bigdata.com to Yahoo Finance (same v1 rules; about 10 of the 100 universe stocks differ, so strength ranks shift slightly); once both periods have results, check that they look alike before treating them as one sample.

2b. Historical backtest (context, not proof). From the same repo, read data/backtest/summary.json, grid.csv and forward_study.json (rebuilt on the 1st of each month by the "Backtest v1 rules" GitHub Action: 10 years of weekly v1 screens on today's S&P 500 members, plus a vectorbt replica of the paper engine across 36 rule variants). Report the baseline's CAGR, max drawdown and Sharpe vs SPY, and for each live-log threshold question from (h) what the 10-year history says (n, weeks, average excess). The backtest has survivorship bias (optimistic) and 36 variants were tried (the best one is partly luck), so: a rule change still needs the live evidence bar in step 3, and the backtest may only support it, or argue against it when the two disagree. Prefer a change only if neighbouring settings in the grid also beat the baseline. If data/paper/metrics.json exists, use it for the paper book's returns and drawdowns.

3. Evidence bar. With fewer than 30 closed trades, say plainly that the paper results are not yet statistically meaningful. For the screener log, a comparison counts only when each side has at least 30 rows with a 4-week result spread over at least 6 distinct weeks; prefer 13-week results once they exist. Give at most 2 observations to keep watching, and recommend no rule changes unless something is clearly broken (a bug, a rule that never fires) or a comparison meets that bar AND the paper trades don't contradict it. Recommend at most 3 specific rule changes in total, each with the numbers it is based on (n, distinct weeks, average excess on each side) and the expected effect. Example: "raise the strength gate from 60 to 70: passers ranked 60-69 averaged -1.8% vs the S&P at 4 weeks across 46 rows from 9 weeks, vs +0.9% for 70+." The insider flag may be proposed as a score input or tie-breaker only if flagged buys have at least 10 trades or 30 logged picks and beat Neutral on both average R and excess return; otherwise report it as still being tested. Do not suggest moving real money to calls on modeled results: with 30+ pairs, the most you can recommend is getting real option quotes to confirm what the model shows. If a screener change is later approved by Cade, it should be applied as a new rules version (v2, v3 ...) with a changelog entry, so results stay comparable — say this when recommending.

4. Save the review: ArtifactData set on the paper dashboard, collection "reviews", doc_id = YYYY-MM, data {date, closed_trades, screener_rows_checked_4w, summary, findings: [..], recommendations: [..]}.

Message Cade (SendUserMessage), under 220 words: performance vs SPY, the key findings (including one line on the screener's learning log — whether finalists are beating the stocks it passed over and failed, or that it's too early — one line on the insider flag and one on shares vs calls), and the recommendations (or "not enough data yet"). End by asking whether he wants any recommended change applied to the screener or paper engines. Paper results are simulated, option prices are modeled, and none of this is financial advice.
